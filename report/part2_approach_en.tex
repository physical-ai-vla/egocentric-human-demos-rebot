\begin{abstract}
We isolate the simplest transferable behaviour, the right arm approaching a red cube, learned from egocentric human
demonstrations only. IMU visual--inertial scale is unobservable on these slow motions, so scale comes from a 3.8\,cm cube or
from the table plane at a taped origin. A shared task frame, a robot wrist hand-eye calibration (0.16\,px RMS) and robot-camera
rendering put human labels in the robot's frame. Models trained on 80--150 takes overfit from the first checkpoint. Robot runs
logged stop reasons but no outcomes, so we report no success rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
The operator wears one HandUMI unit~\cite{chi2024umi} (wrist fisheye, IMU, jaw encoder) on the right hand; wrist poses come from
MASt3R-SLAM~\cite{murai2025mast3rslam} (Part I). Labels use Part I's Cartesian contract (16 relative poses, 50.05\,ms apart, 15\,Hz
rows); the left arm and the gripper are dummies masked out of the loss, so 9 of 20 dimensions are supervised. The model is X-VLA
(0.9B)~\cite{zheng2025xvla} with Part I's recipe (soft-prompt slot 20, batch 8, 8-bit AdamW, bf16), trained on an RTX~4090. The
robot is the reBot B601 right arm with a wrist C922; the left arm and both jaws are locked during deployment.

\section{Metric Scale Without the IMU}
\textbf{HRA\_red.} 200 takes of ``approach the red cube'' (10\,s) were recorded on 3 October. The per-episode IMU scale has a
median of 0.012 and is zero or negative in 95 takes (Fig.~\ref{fig:cube}b): slow approaches do not excite the accelerometer. \textbf{Cube PnP.} The
static cube (3.8\,cm edge) is fitted with two/three-face or near-frontal single-face models and the trajectory scale follows in
closed form from the cube staying put. On bimanual HRL80 takes, where the IMU is reliable, 37 of 57 are PnP-valid with a PnP/IMU
ratio of 1.062 (p16--p84 0.959--1.274; Fig.~\ref{fig:cube}a). Scale and sanity gates keep 166 of 200 takes (151 train, 20{,}319 rows; 15 val); median
scale 0.354 (p5 0.188, p95 0.500), cube-centre residual 0.17\,cm.

\section{Putting Human Data in the Robot's Frame}
\textbf{Robot calibration.} The head C922 is calibrated on a checkerboard (RMS 1.05\,px) and located in the robot base by touching
board corners. The robot wrist camera's hand-eye calibration (13 views, 0.16\,px RMS) puts it 169.5\mm behind the TCP with its
optical axis 33\textdegree{} below TCP $x$. The IK stack's TCP lies about 82\mm ahead of the physical jaw tip; contact now uses
the URDF fingertip box, and earlier ``tip below the table'' readings were this artefact and were retracted. Linking HRA\_red takes
to the base after the fact through the head camera reached only 64 of 166 takes, with centimetre-level height spread, and exposed
an 18\textdegree{} error in the exported camera--IMU extrinsic.

\textbf{Robot-camera rendering (v2).} v1 rendered wrist views with a guessed camera (62--70\textdegree{} field of view); v2 uses
the measured robot camera (52\textdegree) and the hand-eye pose, and expresses labels as robot-TCP poses.

\textbf{Shared origin (HRA\_A100).} Each take starts with the HandUMI jaw tip held for 2\,s on a taped cross that the robot jaw
tip also touched, then a 3\,s move to a robot-like start and a 10\,s approach. Three robot touches on the tape line define the task
frame G (collinear within 0.38\mm over 60.4\mm; table at $z=-27.1$\mm). At the origin the table plane in the first MASt3R
pointmap gives a second scale source (ratio to cube PnP 0.999, per-take error p50 8\%, p90 37\%), and the origin yaw is
measured per take (error p50 0.21\textdegree). 107 takes were kept on 6 October. A table-aware conversion (fingertip floor at
table + 5\mm, endpoint lift at most 30\mm) keeps 86 (CT5/CT6), with fingertip clearance p50 12.6\mm, minimum 7.6\mm.

\section{Robot Runs and Deployment}
v1 moved towards the cube from nearby starts and dragged along the table for far cubes (about 39 and 26\,cm in two runs). On 7
October the operator saw the arm push the cube, so the interface now stops when the cube's apparent size in the wrist view exceeds
a threshold (a proximity proxy, not verified contact): 69 of the day's 127 sessions ended this way. Execution settings mattered as
much as checkpoints: an interface restart that silently reset the chunk-blending parameters made the operator report that
inference quality ``had dropped a lot'' until they were restored. No trial outcome was logged, so there is no success rate.

\section{Lessons and Next Steps}
Each scale source fails silently somewhere (IMU on slow motion, origin plane on some takes), so two independent sources are
needed; a physical origin shared with the robot beats post-hoc registration; the IK TCP is a convention, not the fingertip; small
single-task sets overfit at once, so early checkpoints are the ones to test; and execution settings belong to the policy. Next:
logged trials with fixed settings from fixed start poses and cube positions, early checkpoints of A, B and CT7, and correcting
the cube colour of the rendered images (orange in training, red on the robot).
